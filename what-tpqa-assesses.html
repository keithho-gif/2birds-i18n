What the TPQA assesses.

Every funded training provider in Singapore will sooner or later receive a letter about the Training Provider Quality Assessment. The grade decides whether you keep full funding, partial funding, or your registered status at all. Here is how the process works, and how well-run providers stay calm about it.

Reading timeAbout six minutes
FrameworkSWDA · TPGateway

What is the TPQA?

The Training Provider Quality Assessment is the Skills and Workforce Development Agency's (SWDA) audit of a funded training provider as a whole. It looks at the entire organisation, from how you handle learner feedback to how your board signs off on policy.

SWDA calls it a holistic assessment, and the word is accurate. Assessors read your documented procedures and your implementation evidence side by side. If your manual says one thing and your records show another, the grade suffers.

That is the single most useful thing to understand about the TPQA. It does not reward beautiful paperwork. It rewards providers whose paperwork and daily operations agree.

How is it different from the CQC?

SWDA checks providers in two ways. The TPQA reviews the organisation. The Course Quality Check (CQC) zooms in on the actual course materials you teach from, to confirm they still match what SWDA approved.

Materials that have drifted from the approved version are among the most common findings in the sector. A course gets tweaked over the years, a slide here, an assessment there, until it no longer resembles what was submitted. Both audits reward the same habit, which is keeping documents and practice in step.

How does the process run?

The mechanics are fixed, and there are no surprises in the sequence. SWDA notifies a provider roughly two months before the application deadline. Your Management Representative submits the documents through TPGateway. A team of SWDA assessors then spends one day onsite.

There is no application fee. The grade and the assessment report come back through TPGateway.

What do the grades mean?

The outcome determines whether you keep full funding, keep partial funding, or lose your registered status altogether. Fewer than one in ten providers achieve the strongest outcome.

A strong grade is also worth real money beyond the funding itself. Corporate buyers ask about it, and it steadies the business through the years between assessments.

Can you dispute the outcome?

Yes, but the window is tight and the grounds are narrow. A provider has ten working days to request a review, at a fee of $3,052 inclusive of GST. The review considers only whether procedure failed or the findings contradict the evidence examined.

It is not a second hearing of the merits. In practice, this means you prepare well the only time it counts.

What has changed recently?

Two updates are worth noting. Providers holding a four-year EduTrust certification may qualify for the TPQA on a reduced scope. And from 1 December 2025, the assessment item covering the management of marketing activities was updated, which puts your course pages, brochures and campaigns squarely within the audit's reach.

In short, the marketing now has to be built to the same standard as the courseware.

How do you prepare?

Start months before the letter arrives, not weeks. The providers that do well treat the TPQA as a rehearsal of what they already do daily, rather than a scramble to look different for one day.

The work itself is unglamorous. Read your operation honestly against SWDA's criteria. Find the places where the manual and the daily practice have drifted apart. Close those gaps one at a time, and keep the evidence as you go.

Asked often.

Is there a fee to undergo the TPQA?

No. SWDA charges no application fee for the assessment itself. A disputed outcome may be taken to review within ten working days, at a fee of $3,052 inclusive of GST, on narrow procedural grounds.

How much notice does a provider receive?

Roughly two months before the application deadline. The documents go in through TPGateway, and the onsite visit takes one day.

What is the difference between the TPQA and the CQC?

The TPQA reviews the organisation. The CQC reviews the course materials against what SWDA approved. Both reward the same discipline, which is keeping the documents and the daily work in agreement.
